## Where this started

Gaming Manager began nearly ten years ago as a hand-written program, pieced together from YouTube tutorials, Stack Overflow answers and a lot of trial and error on a real machine. It worked, sometimes, and progress was painfully slow.

AI coding assistants changed that. We aren't a big engineering team, and we don't pretend to be. Without AI tools, GMOOCA would not be anywhere near where it is today.

## How we use it

We use AI assistants, mainly Anthropic's Claude, across almost everything we make:

- **Software.** Writing and reviewing code for Gaming Manager, the license server and the SAS Gateway firmware, and writing the tests that check it.
- **Hardware.** Researching parts, reviewing circuit designs and catching mistakes before a board is made.
- **Documentation.** Drafting user guides, setup procedures and the plain-language guides on this site.
- **This website.** Its design, code and much of its writing were produced with AI help.

## What stays with people

AI drafts and suggests. People decide and answer for the result.

- **We decide what gets built** and how it should work for owners and operators.
- **We test on real machines.** Protocol features are proven on machines on our own bench before release, not just in an AI's answer.
- **We review what goes out.** Code, guides and announcements are checked by a person before they ship.
- **We own the mistakes.** If something we publish is wrong, that's on us, not the tool. Tell us at [{{ site.emails.sales }}](mailto:{{ site.emails.sales }}) and we'll fix it.

## AI in the community

When the forums open, **there's no rule against AI-generated content.** AI is a great tool, and we'd be hypocrites to ban it. We ask for one thing: **tag it.**

- If an AI tool wrote or substantially wrote your post, answer, guide or code, say so: a line like *"Drafted with AI help"* or *"AI-generated, checked against my machine"* is enough.
- Check technical advice before you post it when you can. A confident wrong answer can damage a machine or hurt someone working inside one.
- You're responsible for what you post, whoever or whatever wrote the first draft.

Forgot to tag something? No problem. A moderator will ask you to add a label. The point is that readers know what they're looking at.
